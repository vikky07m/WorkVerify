Explainable risk signals, not verdicts

WorkVerify flags inconsistencies for a human to review. It never declares a candidate fraudulent on its own.

  • Document inconsistencies
  • Name / designation mismatches
  • Joining or exit date mismatches
  • Overlapping employments
  • Duplicate documents
  • Low OCR confidence
  • Suspicious file metadata
  • Unusual edit patterns

Consent is the architecture, not a setting

01

No open database. Employers never browse employee documents — every field is requested and approved individually.

02

Documents sit in private storage behind signed, temporary URLs — never a public link.

03

Every request, approval and access is written to an audit log the employee can read too.

04

Consent expires and can be revoked. Retention and deletion are configurable, not permanent by default.

Where WorkVerify fits

Portable employment records aren't a new idea — Datagate, EmployDB and OnGrid work in the same space. What's different here is the combination: a consent-first wallet, an explainable risk layer, and an integration point for the ATS and HRMS platforms companies already use — rather than one more standalone BGV vendor.

The goal isn't to replace background checks entirely. It's to make the second, third and tenth check nearly instant.
